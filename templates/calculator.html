⚖️ WHAT IS PATENT TERM ADJUSTMENT?
📌
Patent Term Adjustment (PTA) extends a patent's 20-year term to compensate for delays caused by the USPTO during examination, under 35 U.S.C. 154(b).
35 USC 154(b) · 37 CFR 1.702–1.705
📋
Applies to: Utility and plant patent applications filed on or after May 29, 2000. Does NOT apply to design patents.
🧮
Formula: Expiration = min(Filing Date + 20 Years + PTA Days, Terminal Disclaimer Cap Date)
If no TD: Expiration = Filing + 20yr + PTA
📊 PTA DELAY CATEGORIES
🅐
A-Delay (37 CFR 1.702(a)): USPTO failed to act within specific timeframes — 14-month first OA, 4-month responses, 4-month issue after NOA.
37 CFR 1.702(a)(1)–(3)
🅑
B-Delay (37 CFR 1.702(b)): USPTO failed to issue the patent within 3 years of filing (stops at first RCE).
37 CFR 1.702(b)
🅒
C-Delay (37 CFR 1.702(c)–(e)): Secrecy order, interference/derivation proceeding, or successful appellate review.
37 CFR 1.702(c)(d)(e)
➖
Applicant Delay (37 CFR 1.704): Delays caused by the applicant (late responses, extensions of time, supplemental replies) are subtracted from PTA.
37 CFR 1.704(b)(c)
💰 MAINTENANCE FEE RULES
📅
Utility patents filed on/after May 12, 1980 require three maintenance fees at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years after grant. Design and plant patents are exempt.
35 USC 41(b) · 37 CFR 1.362
⏱️
Each fee has a 6-month payment window followed by a 6-month grace period with surcharge. Total window = 12 months per fee.
⚠️
Failure to pay causes immediate patent expiration. Revival requires an "unintentional" petition filed within 2 years of the grace period end.
🔄
Micro/Small entity discounts apply to maintenance fees. PTA does NOT extend maintenance fee due dates — they remain anchored to the grant date.
